# PhysioCheck — Product Overview

**"One platform for comprehensive physiotherapy assessment."**

---

## What is PhysioCheck?

PhysioCheck is a web-based application designed for physiotherapists to conduct structured patient assessments digitally. The therapist selects a physiotherapy specialty, and the system presents a complete assessment form with all relevant sections — subjective assessment, history, physical examination, special tests, and outcome measures. It tracks patient progress over time and generates professional PDF reports.

---

## Who is it for?

- Physiotherapy students learning clinical assessment
- Practicing physiotherapists who want a faster, standardized assessment workflow
- Clinical supervisors who want consistent documentation from their team

---

## How does it work? (User Journey)

### Step 1: Sign Up & Log In

The therapist creates an account with their name, email, and password. After logging in, they land on their personal dashboard.

### Step 2: Add a Patient

The therapist creates a patient profile by entering:
- Patient name
- Age
- Gender
- Diagnosis
- Date

This profile is saved and can be accessed anytime for future assessments.

### Step 3: Start an Assessment

From the patient's profile, the therapist clicks "New Assessment" and sees **10 specialty cards** to choose from:

1. Orthopaedic
2. Neurological
3. Cardiopulmonary
4. Paediatric
5. Geriatric
6. Sports
7. Women's Health
8. Hand Rehabilitation
9. Burns Rehabilitation
10. Amputee Rehabilitation

### Step 4: Fill the Assessment Form

After selecting a specialty, the therapist gets a **complete assessment page** with all sections relevant to that specialty. Every page starts with common fields (patient details, pain assessment using VAS and NPRS), followed by specialty-specific sections.

**Example — Orthopaedic Specialty Page:**

**Common Fields (top of every specialty page):**
- Patient details (name, age, gender, diagnosis)
- Pain Assessment — VAS (0–10), NPRS (0–10)

**Section 1 — Assessment (Subjective):**
- Pain, Stiffness, Swelling, Instability, Locking, Clicking, Giving way, Functional limitations

**Section 2 — History:**
- Trauma/mechanism of injury, Previous fracture, Surgery, Immobilization, Sports/work demands

**Section 3 — Physical Examination:**
- *Observation:* Posture, Swelling, Deformity, Muscle wasting, Scar, Redness, Bruising, Limb alignment
- *Palpation:* Temperature, Tenderness, Swelling, Bony landmarks, Muscle spasm
- *ROM:* Active ROM, Passive ROM, End-feel, Painful arc
- *Muscle Examination:* MMT, Isometric strength, Functional strength
- *Neurological Screening:* Dermatomes, Myotomes, Reflexes, Neural tension
- *Functional Assessment:* Walking, Stairs, Sit-to-stand, Squat, ADLs

**Section 4 — Major Special Tests:**
- *Knee:* Lachman, Anterior drawer, Posterior drawer, McMurray, Thessaly, Varus stress, Valgus stress, Patellar apprehension
- *Shoulder:* Neer, Hawkins-Kennedy, Empty Can/Jobe, Drop Arm, Apprehension/relocation, Speed's
- *Hip:* FABER, FADIR, Thomas test, Trendelenburg
- *Spine:* SLR, Slump, Prone instability, Spurling, Cervical distraction
- *Ankle:* Anterior drawer, Talar tilt, Thompson test

**Section 5 — Outcome Measures:**
- LEFS, DASH/QuickDASH, SPADI, WOMAC, KOOS, ODI, NDI
- Therapist enters the score they have calculated (e.g., WOMAC: __/96)

The therapist fills in the sections relevant to their patient and submits.

## All 10 Specialties and Their Sections

### 1. Orthopaedic
- Subjective: Pain, Stiffness, Swelling, Instability, Locking, Clicking, Giving way, Functional limitations
- History: Trauma, Previous fracture, Surgery, Immobilization, Sports/work demands
- Examination: Observation, Palpation, ROM, Muscle examination, Neurological screening, Functional assessment
- Special Tests: Knee, Shoulder, Hip, Spine, Ankle tests
- Outcome Measures: LEFS, DASH/QuickDASH, SPADI, WOMAC, KOOS, ODI, NDI

### 2. Neurological
- History: Onset, Progression, Falls, Seizures, Sensory symptoms, Bladder/bowel, Speech/swallowing, Mobility level
- Examination: Mental status, Cranial nerves, Motor examination, Tone, Reflexes, Sensory, Coordination, Balance, Gait
- Special Tests/Scales: Glasgow Coma Scale, Modified Ashworth, Berg Balance, TUG, Functional Reach, 10-Meter Walk, 6-Minute Walk, Romberg, Sharpened Romberg
- Condition-specific: Stroke (Brunnstrom, Fugl-Meyer), SCI (ISNCSCI/ASIA), Parkinsonism (Hoehn & Yahr)

### 3. Cardiopulmonary
- History: Dyspnoea, Cough, Sputum, Chest pain, Exercise tolerance, Smoking history, Cardiac history, Medication
- Examination: Vitals (HR, BP, RR, SpO2, Temperature), Observation, Palpation, Percussion, Auscultation, Exercise assessment
- Tests: 6MWT, 2MWT, Incremental Shuttle Walk, Sit-to-Stand, Borg rating
- Respiratory Measures: Peak flow, Spirometry results, MIP/MEP

### 4. Paediatric
- History: Antenatal, Birth, NICU, Developmental history, Milestones, Family history
- Examination: Observation, Developmental assessment, Neurological (tone, reflexes, primitive reflexes, postural reactions), Musculoskeletal (ROM, deformities, contractures)
- Tests/Scales: GMFCS, GMFM, Modified Ashworth, HINE, AIMS
- Cerebral Palsy specific: Spasticity, Selective motor control, Functional classification

### 5. Geriatric
- History: Falls, Medications, Mobility, ADLs, Previous fractures, Assistive devices
- Examination: Posture, ROM, Strength, Balance, Gait, Transfers, Functional (bed mobility, sit-to-stand, walking, stairs)
- Tests: TUG, Berg Balance, 5 Times Sit-to-Stand, 30-Second Chair Stand, Functional Reach, 6MWT

### 6. Sports
- History: Sport, Position, Training volume, Mechanism of injury, Previous injuries, Return-to-sport goal
- Examination: Observation, ROM, Strength, Flexibility, Movement quality, Functional assessment (squat, single-leg squat, landing, cutting, running mechanics)
- Special Tests: Knee (Lachman, Pivot shift, McMurray), Ankle (Anterior drawer, Talar tilt)
- Functional Tests: Single-leg hop, Triple hop, Crossover hop, Vertical jump, Y-Balance Test, Agility tests

### 7. Women's Health
- History: Obstetric, Pregnancy, Delivery type, Pelvic pain, Urinary/bowel/sexual symptoms (with consent), Menstrual history
- Examination: Posture, Lumbar spine, Pelvic alignment, Musculoskeletal (ROM, strength, abdominal function, breathing), Pelvic floor (with informed consent)
- Tests: Diastasis recti measurement, Pelvic girdle pain provocation, Active Straight Leg Raise
- Outcome Measures: PFDI-20, Pelvic Floor Impact Questionnaire, ICIQ questionnaires

### 8. Hand Rehabilitation
- History: Hand dominance, Occupation, Mechanism of injury, Surgery, Functional limitations
- Examination: Observation (swelling, scar, deformity, atrophy), ROM (wrist, MCP, PIP, DIP), Strength (grip, pinch), Sensory (light touch, two-point discrimination, Semmes-Weinstein monofilaments)
- Special Tests: Carpal tunnel (Phalen, Tinel, Durkan), De Quervain (Finkelstein), Thumb (CMC grind), Tendon gliding assessment

### 9. Burns Rehabilitation
- History: Cause, Date, Depth, Percentage TBSA, Surgery/grafting, Healing stage
- Examination: Skin (scar, pigmentation, adhesions, hypertrophic scar), ROM (joint ROM, contractures), Strength (MMT, functional strength), Function (ADL, mobility, hand function), Pain (resting, movement)
- Scar Assessment: Vancouver Scar Scale, POSAS
- Other: ROM measurement, Contracture assessment, Functional capacity

### 10. Amputee Rehabilitation
- History: Cause, Level, Date, Surgery, Comorbidities, Previous prosthesis
- Examination: Residual limb (shape, length, skin, scar, swelling, tenderness), ROM (hip flexion/abduction, knee flexion contractures), Strength (trunk, hip muscles, remaining limb), Balance (sitting, standing), Functional (transfers, wheelchair, walking)
- Tests: Amputee Mobility Predictor, TUG, 6MWT, 10MWT, Prosthetic fit and alignment observation

## What Happens After Submission

### Scores
The therapist enters outcome measure scores manually. The system stores them for comparison across visits.

### Problem List
Based on the findings filled in the form, the system suggests a problem list. For example:
- Pain 8/10 → "Severe pain"
- ROM reduced → "Reduced joint range of motion"
- MMT Grade 3 → "Muscle weakness"

The therapist can edit this list.

### Goals
The system provides editable goal templates:
- **Short-term:** Reduce pain, Improve ROM, Improve strength
- **Long-term:** Independent walking, Return to ADLs, Return to sport/work

### Progress Tracking
When the therapist conducts follow-up assessments for the same patient:
- Each assessment is stored with its date
- A comparison table shows changes over time (e.g., Pain: 8 → 5 → 3)
- Visual charts show improvement trends

### PDF Report
The therapist can generate a professional PDF report containing:
- Patient details
- All assessment findings
- Outcome measure scores
- Problem list
- Goals
- Therapist notes

## What Makes PhysioCheck Useful?

| Problem Today | How PhysioCheck Solves It |
|---------------|--------------------------|
| Therapists forget which special tests apply to a specialty area | System shows all relevant tests automatically |
| Outcome measure scores are scattered across paper forms | All scores stored digitally, tracked over time |
| Paper assessments are hard to compare across visits | Digital progress tracking with comparison tables and charts |
| No standardized format across therapists | Every assessment follows the same structured format |
| Reports take time to write | One-click PDF report generation |
| Students struggle to remember complete assessment protocols | The system guides them through every section |
